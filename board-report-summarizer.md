# Board Report Summarizer

## What you need to provide

The skill will ask for any of these you didn't include upfront:

- **Reporting period:** e.g., "April 2026" or "Q1 FY26"
- **Program activities:** what happened, how many served, what changed
- **Financial snapshot:** cash position, YTD revenue vs. budget, YTD expenses vs. budget, any variance worth flagging
- **Wins:** 2–3 specific things that went well (with names, numbers, or outcomes attached)
- **Challenges / risks:** what's not working, what's at risk, what staff is worried about
- **Decisions needed from the board:** anything requiring a vote, approval, or formal input this meeting
- **Anything personnel-related:** hiring, departures, or sensitive items the board needs to know

## How to do it

1. **Lead with decisions, not activity.** If the board needs to vote on something or approve something, that goes at the very top. Before any program update. Their time is most valuable when they're deciding, not absorbing.

2. **Default structure:**
   - **Decisions Needed This Meeting:** bullet list, one line each, with what's being asked
   - **Executive Summary:** 3–4 sentences. The state of the org right now.
   - **Program Highlights:** 3 specific wins with numbers. Not "we had a great month."
   - **Financial Snapshot:** one paragraph or a small table. Cash on hand, % to budget revenue, % to budget expense, any flag.
   - **Risks and Challenges:** what could derail us. Be specific. "Funding pipeline soft for Q3" beats "fundraising challenges."
   - **Looking Ahead:** 2–3 things happening before next board meeting they should know about.

3. **One page max.** If it doesn't fit, cut. Don't shrink the font.

4. **Use specifics, not adjectives.** "Served 142 families, up 18% from March" beats "served many families this month."

5. **Name people.** If staff or volunteers did something specific, say their first name. Boards remember names.

## Style and voice

- **Voice:** Direct, confident, no hedging. The ED is reporting from a position of competence. No "we tried to" or "hopefully." Say what happened or what's planned.
- **Tone:** Honest about challenges. Boards lose trust faster from sanitized reports than from real ones. If something's not working, name it and say what you're doing about it.
- **Length:** One page. ~350–500 words.
- **Format:** Headers per section. Short paragraphs (2–4 sentences) and bullet lists. Bold the numbers and dates that matter.

## What to watch for and why

These are the patterns the AI gets wrong most often on board reports. Each is paired with the reasoning. When you understand *why* a rule exists, you can recognize the rare cases where it shouldn't apply.

- **Don't bury the lede:** board members open the packet, skim, and decide what to read before the meeting. If decisions they need to make aren't in the first section, those decisions get worse or get deferred. Lead with decisions, even if they feel less interesting than program updates.
- **Don't write the program section as a diary:** date-by-date narration ("on April 3rd we did X, then on April 10th…") is for staff notes; a board scanning the packet won't extract outcomes from it. Compress chronology into outcomes ("served 142 families, up 18% from March").
- **Avoid hedging language:** "we hope to," "we are committed to," "we will strive to" are filler that reads as a lack of conviction. Cut them. State what you'll do or what happened. If the answer is uncertain, say "we expect X by Y, contingent on Z" rather than "we hope."
- **Don't sanitize challenges:** boards lose trust *faster* from a polished report than from an honest one, because polished reports signal that staff thinks the board can't handle the truth. "Slight headwinds in development" gives the board nothing to act on; "two major donors paused giving; here's the plan" gives them what they need to govern.
- **Don't dump financials:** a one-page report is for the snapshot (cash on hand, % to budget, any flag). The full P&L belongs as an attachment, not in the narrative. Including it makes the page unreadable and signals you're hiding the headline in the spreadsheet.
- **Don't pad with mission language:** the board already knows the mission; opening with "As an organization committed to…" wastes the most valuable real estate on the page. Open with what happened or what decision is needed.
- **Don't list every staff activity:** if an activity doesn't change the org's trajectory, the board doesn't need it. Internal updates belong in staff communications, not board reports.

## Example

**Input the user gives:**
> April board update. We served 142 families in our food security program (up from 120 in March). Hired a new part-time outreach coordinator, Maria Rodriguez, started April 14. Cash on hand $87K, about 4.2 months operating. We're 38% to YTD revenue budget but only at month 4, so on track. One big issue: the Smith Foundation grant ($65K we counted on for fall) just got declined. Looking at alternatives. Board needs to approve the updated personnel policy (in their packet) and approve adding a second outreach coordinator role if we can find the funding.

**What the skill should produce:**
> *(One-page summary leading with "Decisions Needed: (1) Approve updated personnel policy, (2) Approve second outreach coordinator role contingent on funding." Executive summary of 3 sentences. Program Highlights leading with the 142-family number and the 18% growth, naming Maria Rodriguez's start date. Financial Snapshot with the $87K / 4.2 months / 38% YTD figures. Risks section names the Smith Foundation decline specifically with a one-sentence plan. Looking Ahead with what's between now and next meeting. ~400 words, no buzzwords, no hedging.)*
