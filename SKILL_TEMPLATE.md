## What you need to provide

List exactly what info the AI needs from the user to do the job well. The clearer this list, the better the output:

- [Input 1. E.g., "The program description, including who it serves and what activities you run"]
- [Input 2. E.g., "The funder name and what they prioritize (paste from their RFP or website)"]
- [Input 3. E.g., "The word or character count limit"]

## How to do it

Write each step in **imperative form** ("Open the program description," not "You should open the program description"). Same way you'd write any SOP for a new hire.

After each step, briefly explain the **why** behind it. The reasoning matters: when the AI understands *why* a step matters, it can handle the weird cases your steps don't cover. When you only give the rule with no reasoning, it breaks at the first edge case.

1. First, [do X]. [Why this matters.]
2. Then, [do Y]. [Why this matters.]
3. Finally, [do Z]. [Why this matters.]

## What to watch for and why

This is the most valuable section. List the patterns the AI is likely to get wrong on this task. And pair each one with the *reasoning*. Update this section every time you run the skill and the output isn't right.

Avoid rigid "never do this" rules with no explanation. The AI is smart; when you tell it *why* something is wrong, it can apply the principle in cases you didn't anticipate, and recognize the rare situations where the rule shouldn't apply. Pure prohibitions without reasoning can hamstring it.

- **Don't [common mistake 1]:** because [reasoning]. Instead, [what to do].
- **Don't [common mistake 2]:** because [reasoning]. Instead, [what to do].
- **Don't [common mistake 3]:** because [reasoning]. Instead, [what to do].
